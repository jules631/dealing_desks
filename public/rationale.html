PM Rationale

Why this product exists, and what building it taught me.

Deal Desk AI started as a conviction: that the conversation is the source of truth for any enterprise deal, and that making AEs re-encode that conversation into forms is the root cause of quote errors, weak justifications, and approval delays. This is the product rationale, design decisions, and honest post-build notes.

01 — The problem with how deals get priced today

45–90
minutes an AE spends translating one call into a CPQ quote
~40%
of submitted quotes require at least one revision before approval
7–12
days the average enterprise deal loses to slow approval cycles

Every enterprise software deal runs on conversations. A call, a Slack thread, a few emails. Inside those conversations is everything you need to price and justify a deal: company size, budget signals, timeline pressure, competitive context, who has authority. All of it is there. Almost none of it makes it into the CPQ.

Current CPQ tools are form-first. They start with fields. The AE has to translate the conversation into those fields manually, usually after the fact, usually in a hurry. The translation is lossy by design. The quote emerges misconfigured. The justification is boilerplate that doesn't reflect the specific deal signals that make the discount defensible.

"The problem isn't that AEs don't know the deal. It's that the tools make them re-encode what they know into a format that destroys the context."

The result is a cascade: revision cycles, rejected approvals, and deals that slip because the internal approval process took longer than the prospect's decision window. The cost isn't just the time — it's the deals that close at a lower price because the AE couldn't substantiate the ask, or don't close at all.

02 — Why the translation layer is now solvable

The gap has always been the translation layer — turning unstructured conversation into structured deal configuration. What's changed is that language models can now do this translation with genuine understanding, not key-phrase matching.

Tool What it misses
Salesforce CPQ Starts with fields. No conversation layer. Justification is AE-authored from scratch with no grounding in the actual call.
DealHub Guided selling by rules, not conversation. Outputs are templated — not grounded in the specific deal signals that make the discount defensible.
Logik.ai Solves configuration complexity, not the translation from conversation to configuration. Still requires an AE who has already encoded the deal.
Gong / Chorus Surfaces conversation intelligence retrospectively for coaching. Does not produce a quote or a justification. Different job to be done.
Deal Desk AI Conversation → structured intelligence → configured quote → grounded justification. The translation layer, finally automated.

Claude, specifically, has two properties that make it right for this use case. First, it distinguishes between what the prospect said directly and what can be inferred — which maps naturally to the Explicit/Implied signal taxonomy this tool surfaces. Second, the approval justifications it drafts sound like an experienced AE wrote them. Specific, confident, grounded in the actual conversation — not summarization boilerplate.

The white space is the translation layer. Every AE does this manually today. It's a 45-minute task that produces mediocre output and leaves value on the table — both in the quote configuration and in the justification quality.

03 — Key design decisions and the reasoning behind them

Why a textarea, not a form?
The AE's native artifact is the conversation — an email thread, call notes, a forwarded Slack message. Making them re-encode that into fields before we can help them defeats the purpose. We read the conversation. We produce the structure.
Why Explicit vs. Implied confidence?
Not all signals are equal. "Budget is $18k/month, approved" is different from "they seem budget-constrained." The distinction matters for how much weight the justification can put on each signal. We surface this so the AE can decide how to represent the deal — we don't hide uncertainty inside confident-sounding prose.
Why not auto-submit the justification?
The AE still reads, edits, and submits the justification. We draft it grounded in the actual signals; they own the final word. Deal desk analysts are experienced at spotting boilerplate — and at spotting when an AE oversold context they didn't actually have. Human in the loop is a feature.
Why the Simulate Deal Desk Review feature?
The second AI pass acts as a skeptical analyst stress-testing the justification before it goes to approval. The pushback questions it returns are specific to this deal — not generic challenges. This is the difference between submitting something that holds up and submitting something that bounces back on day three of a Q-end week.
Why a pricing model toggle, not AI-inferred only?
The AE sometimes has a motion in mind before the conversation analysis runs — a consumption pilot they've promised, or a seat deal the company requires for procurement compliance. Letting the AE set context before the analysis means the AI's recommendations align with the actual sales motion, not just the conversation signals.

04 — What building the prototype taught me

01
Structured output is both the product and the hardest engineering challenge.

The AI has to return a JSON object with a specific schema — signals with confidence labels, products with pricing calculations, a risk rating grounded in policy. Getting the model to be consistently precise, especially on discount math and volume tier logic, required a very specific system prompt. The instruction to "never invent signals" and to "ground every justification claim in a specific quote from the conversation" was the most important prompt engineering decision in the whole project.

02
The conversation-first constraint is load-bearing, not aesthetic.

Early in design, I considered adding an optional form for AEs who "prefer structure." Dropping it was the right call. The moment you add form fields, you're back in CPQ-land — the AE is re-encoding the deal instead of feeding context. The textarea forces the product to earn its value by reading unstructured text. That constraint is what makes the prototype interesting.

03
Two-call architecture doubles latency but improves quality significantly.

The deal desk simulation is a second API call with a separate system prompt — a skeptical analyst reviewing the justification rather than an analyst generating it. Running these as a single prompt produced weaker pushback questions; the role separation matters. The added latency (roughly 4–8 seconds) is acceptable because simulation is opt-in and happens after the primary analysis, not on the critical path.

04
The approval justification is the highest-value output — and the hardest to evaluate.

Quote configuration is verifiable: the math either matches policy or it doesn't. Signal extraction is assessable: you can compare the signals to the conversation and check for hallucinations. The justification is harder — it has to be confident, specific, AE-voiced, and grounded without being generic. The instruction to write it "in the voice of an experienced AE — confident, specific, business-justified, not generic" produced noticeably better output than asking for a "summary."

05 — What comes next if this becomes a real product

Validation
Run a structured pilot with 10–15 AEs

Baseline the current time-from-call-to-quote-submission and first-pass approval rate. Measure against those metrics — not just sentiment. High reuse of the AI justification draft with minimal editing is the leading indicator that the output is genuinely useful.

Integration
Gong transcript ingestion

The current input is a manual paste. The natural next step is reading Gong/Chorus transcripts directly, eliminating the copy-paste step and giving the AI richer signal from the full call rather than whatever the AE remembered to put in their notes.

Catalog
Live product catalog via CRM sync

The current catalog is hardcoded. A real version would pull pricing, discount policy, and approval thresholds from the CRM or product catalog API. The discount risk logic and VP approval flags should reflect current policy, not a snapshot baked into the system prompt.

Workflow
One-click CPQ prefill

The current output is a copyable justification. The high-value completion is writing the CPQ fields directly — product, quantity, pricing model, discount — via Salesforce CPQ or DealHub API. The AE reviews, edits if needed, and submits. The translation step disappears entirely.

The bet is that the conversation is the source of truth for the deal, and that extracting structure from it — rather than asking the AE to encode structure into forms — produces better outcomes: more accurate quotes, more defensible justifications, fewer revision cycles, faster closes.
